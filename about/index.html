How the cost estimates are calculated

Every estimate is the sum of individual services. Each service is priced from its provider's published rates and from the usage your answers imply. The prices come from official pricing pages checked on 1 November 2026; the usage comes from default assumptions you can edit in the calculator.

Updated 2 November 2026 · Published by Smart Project Cost Calculator

How do your answers become usage?

  • Monthly active users = registered users × the share active each month (25% for occasional use up to 70% for business-critical use). Registered and active users are not the same.
  • API requests = monthly active users × sessions per user × requests per session. Sessions run from 4 a month (occasional) to 50 (heavy use); requests per session depend on the project type.
  • Database operations = API requests × database operations per request.
  • Bandwidth = page views × page weight + API responses + file downloads.
  • Peak traffic = average requests per second × a peak multiplier (2 to 5). Servers are sized for the peak, not the average.
  • AI requests = monthly active users × AI requests per user (about 15, 60 or 250 a month for light, medium or heavy use).

How is usage turned into cost?

  • Usage charges: cost = max(0, usage − free allowance) ÷ billing unit × unit price. Tiered and volume prices are applied band by band.
  • Servers: cost = hourly price × 730 hours × instances. Instances = peak requests per second ÷ the assumed capacity of one instance, rounded up.
  • Storage: cost = billable GB × price per GB per month.
  • AI models: cost = input tokens ÷ 1,000,000 × input price + cached tokens ÷ 1,000,000 × cache-read price + output tokens ÷ 1,000,000 × output price.
  • Annual cost = monthly cost × 12. One-time fees (such as the Google Play registration) are shown separately as setup cost.

How are the three architectures chosen?

For each part of the stack the calculator considers a family of plans from one provider and picks the cheapest plan whose limits fit your usage. Free plans that do not allow commercial use, or that pause inactive projects, are only offered for testing. Budget uses free tiers and serverless services. Recommended uses paid, production-ready plans that a small team can run. Scalable adds autoscaling, caching and paid monitoring only when your traffic or reliability priority justifies them. The option that fits your priority and budget is highlighted.

What is not included?

  • Taxes such as GST or VAT.
  • Payment gateway fees, because they are a percentage of your revenue (for example Razorpay's 2% or Stripe's 2.9% + $0.30 in the US).
  • Developer time, support plans, reserved-capacity discounts and negotiated rates. You can enter your own prices in the calculator's advanced mode.
  • Regional price differences: cloud prices are for the region shown on each service (mostly US regions).
