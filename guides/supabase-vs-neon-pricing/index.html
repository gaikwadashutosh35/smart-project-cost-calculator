Supabase vs Neon pricing: what you'll actually pay in 2026

On Supabase vs Neon pricing, the short answer is that Neon is cheaper for small or mostly idle databases, because its compute scales to zero and there is no monthly minimum. For an always-on app with tens of thousands of users the two land within about $10 of each other. At 150,000 users Neon came out far cheaper in our test — $128.38 against $293.50 a month — mainly because Supabase charges for monthly active users above 100,000 while Neon's built-in auth is free up to 1,000,000.

How the compute sizes were matched

Each pair has the same memory: Supabase Micro (1 GB) against a 0.25 CU Neon compute, Small (2 GB) against 0.5 CU, and Medium (4 GB) against 1 CU. The side project's Neon database is awake about 120 hours a month and sleeps the rest; every other scenario runs 730 hours, all month. Neon's instant-restore history ($0.20 per GB-month) and Supabase file storage are left out because they depend on settings rather than users.

Supabase vs Neon free tier

Both free plans can run a small side project for $0. Neon Free gives each project 1 GB of storage, 100 CU-hours of compute and 5 GB of egress, and it never pauses a project for inactivity — when a limit runs out, compute is suspended until the next month. Supabase Free gives a 500 MB database, 5 GB of egress, 1 GB of file storage and 50,000 monthly active users, but pauses a project after one week without activity and allows two active projects.

The practical difference: 100 CU-hours covers a 0.25 CU database for about 400 hours, so Neon Free suits apps that sleep most of the day. Supabase Free suits anything with users signing in, as long as you are not relying on it in production.

Where the two bills differ

Compute: a fixed instance against CU-hours

Supabase charges for an instance that is always on: Micro is included in the $25 Pro fee, Small adds $5, Medium $50 and Large $100 a month. Neon charges $0.106 per CU-hour only while the database is awake. A 0.25 CU database that never sleeps uses 182.5 CU-hours a month, which costs $19.35; one that sleeps overnight costs less.

Storage: Neon costs more per gigabyte

Supabase includes 8 GB and then charges $0.125 per GB; Neon charges $0.35 per GB-month from the first gigabyte. A 100 GB database adds $11.50 a month on Supabase and $35 on Neon.

Egress: Neon includes twice as much

Supabase Pro includes 250 GB and then charges $0.09 per GB; Neon Launch includes 500 GB per project and then charges $0.10 per GB. Sending 1,000 GB a month costs $67.50 extra on Supabase and $50 on Neon.

Authentication: included on both, with different ceilings

Supabase Pro includes 100,000 monthly active users and charges $0.00325 for each one above that, so 150,000 users add $162.50 a month. Neon's Managed Better Auth has no separate charge up to 1,000,000 monthly active users per project on paid plans (60,000 on Free). This is the largest single difference at high user counts.

What if you run several projects or environments?

Supabase charges one Pro fee per organisation, and each project after the first needs its own compute, from $10 a month for Micro. Five small Supabase projects therefore cost about $65 a month ($25 plus four Micro instances), not five times $25. On Neon each project is billed only for the compute it uses, so staging and preview databases that sleep most of the time cost little: five 0.25 CU projects awake 100 hours a month each use 125 CU-hours, about $13 of compute. Neon also includes 10 branches per project on Launch, then charges $1.50 per branch-month; Supabase branching costs $0.01344 per branch-hour.

Which is cheaper for your app?

  • Choose Neon on cost if your database is idle much of the time, if you run many small or preview environments, or if you expect more than 100,000 monthly active users.
  • Choose Supabase on cost if you run an always-on app with tens of thousands of users and a larger database, where its included 8 GB of storage and cheaper storage rate pay off.
  • Count what you would otherwise buy. Supabase Pro also includes 100 GB of file storage, realtime messaging and 2 million Edge Function invocations; on Neon you may pay for those elsewhere.
  • Need SOC 2, SSO or SLAs? Supabase's Team plan starts at $599 a month; Neon's Scale plan raises compute to $0.222 per CU-hour.

Frequently asked questions

Is Neon cheaper than Supabase?

It depends on usage. In our four scenarios Neon was cheaper for a mostly idle side project, a 10,000-user startup and a 150,000-user app ($128.38 vs $293.50 a month), while Supabase was cheaper for an always-on app with 50,000 users ($36 vs $45.69).

What does a Neon CU-hour cost?

$0.106 per CU-hour on the Launch plan and $0.222 on Scale. One CU is about 4 GB of memory, so a 0.25 CU database that never sleeps uses 182.5 CU-hours a month, which costs $19.35 on Launch.

Does Neon include authentication?

Yes. Neon's Managed Better Auth has no separate charge up to 60,000 monthly active users per project on the Free plan and 1,000,000 on paid plans. Supabase Pro includes 100,000 monthly active users, then charges $0.00325 per user.

Supabase vs Neon free tier: which gives more?

Neon Free gives 1 GB of storage and 100 CU-hours of compute per project and never pauses for inactivity. Supabase Free gives a 500 MB database and up to 50,000 monthly active users but pauses projects after a week without activity. Both include 5 GB of egress.

Do Supabase and Neon charge for egress?

Both include some. Supabase Pro includes 250 GB a month, then charges $0.09 per GB. Neon Launch includes 500 GB per project, then charges $0.10 per GB.
